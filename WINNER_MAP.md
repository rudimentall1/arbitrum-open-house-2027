# Arbitrum hackathon and Open House winner map

Research snapshot: 26 November 2026.

## Scope and limits

This map covers the major publicly documented Arbitrum Open House winner sets from India 2025 through Singapore 2026, plus selected Arbitrum-specific hackathons from 2023–2024. It separates online Buildathon results from in-person Founder House results because they are different competitions. Some event archives list only project names; where a description was not available from a reliable source, it is marked as not detailed here.

This is not a complete census of every ETHGlobal event that offered an Arbitrum prize. It is a focused map of the main Arbitrum-run or Arbitrum-specific winner cohorts relevant to Open House São Paulo 2027.

## Cross-event analysis

### Repeated winning clusters

1. **Liquidity and trading primitives:** AMMs, DEXs, perps, routing, market-making, and options.
2. **Tokenized assets and credit:** tokenized stocks, RWA yield, credit pools, exits, collateral, and equity infrastructure.
3. **Risk and operations:** risk ratings, hedging, credit underwriting, escrow, and financial control layers.
4. **Agentic finance:** agents that trade, manage vaults, research DeFi, or coordinate financial actions.
5. **Privacy and compliance:** cross-chain privacy, FHE, passkeys, and insurance claims.
6. **Emerging-market finance:** multicurrency accounts, wage-linked finance, and region-specific insurance.

### What not to clone for São Paulo 2027

- Generic AMM, DEX, or perpetuals venue.
- Tokenized-stock lending, long/short, options, or index-token product.
- Generic RWA yield vault.
- Generic AI trading or vault-management agent.
- Generic escrow for high-value purchases.
- Generic cross-chain privacy pool.
- Generic wallet or agent control layer.

These areas have multiple recent winners and established competitors.

### Less represented workflow to investigate

**Post-origination servicing of tokenized private credit.**

Recent winners have targeted tokenization, liquidity, collateral, credit exits, risk ratings, and yield. A potential adjacent workflow is the operational work after a loan has been issued: reconciling borrower cashflows and repayment records with onchain pool activity, tracking arrears, calculating distributions, and preparing investor reports.

This is not proven whitespace. Traditional loan-servicing and fund-administration vendors, as well as tokenization platforms, already serve parts of the lifecycle. Validate a narrow cross-ledger workflow with actual operators before building.

## Final bounded validation cycle — 27 November 2026

The existing post-origination servicing of tokenized private credit hypothesis is the only adjacent workflow authorized for Pass B of the final validation cycle. It is **not** a selected product or a validated whitespace claim. Research only with actual loan servicers, private-credit fund operators, or tokenization platform operations teams: identify borrower payment reconciliation, arrears/default handling, waterfall/distribution calculations, investor reporting, and the systems currently used. Require recurring manual work, measurable cost/risk, an economic buyer, and a bounded pilot path. Do not add adjacent segments without replacing a weaker finalist.

Discovery is capped by D-025: Pass A operator validation (RPC/indexer integrity and liquidation send-time gap), Pass B this one existing workflow, Pass C final scoring and GO/NO-GO. Maximum three finalists. If none meets the GO gate, stop the current search set.


## Final validation outcome — D-026 (27 November 2026)

The post-origination servicing hypothesis was tested in Pass B and **not selected**. General private-credit servicing/reconciliation pain is credible, but servicing/accounting integrations and tokenization platforms already cover much of the proposed workflow. No tokenized-credit-specific buyer, independently quantified cost, unmet workflow, or bounded pilot commitment was validated. D-026 records NO-GO for the current search set. This hypothesis remains historical research context, not a GO candidate. See [research/PASS_B_AND_FINAL_NO_GO_2026-11-27.md](research/PASS_B_AND_FINAL_NO_GO_2026-11-27.md).
