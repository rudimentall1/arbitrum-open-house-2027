# Global niche scan 02 — 27 November 2026

## Executive result

**No new GO candidate. No implementation authorized.** This is a continuation of the user's request to search for another Buildathon niche while the three earlier outreach threads remain active. Only one of the three D-029 commodity-collateral outreach attempts was successfully sent; the other two LinkedIn profiles did not accept messages. This scan does not treat that access failure as a market rejection.

The scan screened six additional problem spaces:
1. construction-loan progress vs subcontractor-payment verification;
2. rented GPU / AI compute SLA evidence and dispute resolution;
3. durable carbon-removal offtake delivery risk;
4. EU CBAM supplier emissions-data collection and verification;
5. voluntary biodiversity-credit integrity and market infrastructure;
6. ocean freight demurrage/detention invoice disputes.

All six were rejected as generic Buildathon product candidates because either a directly matching product / official infrastructure already exists, or the market lacks demonstrated buyer demand and stable standards. Several contain real pain, but pain alone does not establish a differentiated product opportunity.

## Evidence and source notes

### 1. Construction lending

IPEX's own product page states that it verifies subcontractor/supplier payments alongside QS-certified progress, identifies payment gaps, validates cost-to-complete and can add controls around builder withdrawals. This is almost exactly the obvious product hypothesis, so it is not a defensible greenfield opportunity.

Sources:
- https://ipex.com.au/construction-lending-payment-verification/
- https://ipex.com.au/solutions/lenders/
- https://www.finantrix.com/buyer-guides/construction-loan-management-software-lenders
- https://www.moraph.com/solutions/construction-loan-draw-management
- https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/cbem.pdf

### 2. GPU cloud SLA evidence

A public October 2026 project named GPU Sentinel describes signed provider/tenant evidence logs, an SLA engine, event corroboration and a shared ledger. Its README explicitly says it is pre-alpha, based on synthetic data, and has not been validated on production GPU clusters. This is not proof the market is solved, but it is a direct competing project with essentially the same hackathon thesis. Do not copy it.

Sources:
- https://github.com/Sievehouse/gpu-sentinel
- https://kernelmachine.com/guides/why-gpu-rentals-fail
- https://racktrail.com/five-places-new-gpu-clouds-struggle/

### 3. Carbon-removal delivery risk

The gap between contracted and delivered removals is real, but delivery is already tracked by market intelligence and procurement platforms. Contractual controls also directly address the problem: milestone-based payments, delivery-linked payment, replacement credits, refund provisions and termination rights. A new ledger or escrow contract would not solve project construction/technology failure or create enforceable recourse.

Sources:
- https://www.cdr.fyi/resources/cdr-buyers-guide/cdr-buyers-guide/access-supply/purchase-types
- https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/types-of-cdr-purchases-buyers-guide
- https://frontierclimate.com/progress?delivery_status=delivered
- https://senseible.earth/answers/carbon-removal-forward-contract-milestones
- https://www.msci.com/data-and-analytics/carbon-markets/understanding-carbon-markets/delivery-risks

### 4. CBAM

The European Commission's own CBAM Registry includes declarant reporting and a Third Country Installation portal (O3CI) through which non-EU producers can upload/share installation emissions data with multiple declarants. Actual emissions data must be verified by accredited independent verifiers. This creates a workflow burden but also means the public infrastructure and commercial software market are already forming.

### 5. Biodiversity credits

Market infrastructure and methodologies are immature, but the limiting factor is currently demand rather than a missing blockchain registry. The 2026 market survey of 52 actors reports that 15 of 19 current sellers rated demand weak/very weak and 18 of 19 said lack of demand constrained sales. OECD puts voluntary biodiversity-credit sales at approximately USD 6M as of April 2026.

### 6. Ocean freight D&D

The US FMC rule defines minimum invoice fields and dispute processes. Numerous commercial products already track containers, free time, detention/demurrage exposure and invoice disputes. This is an established logistics-software category, not a clean Arbitrum opportunity.

## What this means

- No new product should be started from this scan.
- Do not revive D-026 or D-028, and do not repackage Verigate.
- Do not clone GPU Sentinel or IPEX.
- The D-029 commodity-collateral hypothesis remains WATCH only and independent of this scan; one outreach was sent, two LinkedIn contacts were inaccessible. This is a channel-access problem, not a negative buyer response.
- The next scan should move to distinct problem spaces not yet screened in this pass, preferably with a clear budget owner, direct operational cost, reachable buyer, and a reason for shared Arbitrum state that is not merely immutability.

## Research limitations

This is public-source desk research. No customer interviews, production datasets, quantified buyer-specific costs, paid pilots or willingness-to-pay commitments were obtained for these six niches. Vendor pages describe their own products and should not be treated as independent performance audits. No TAM/SAM/SOM is asserted. No code or tests were changed/run.

## Decision

**D-030: NO-GO for the six generic product theses screened in this report.** Continue global discovery in a new problem frame; do not implement any of these generic products. The report does not reject the underlying operational problems as nonexistent.
