## 1. Aircraft component lifecycle records and transfer

### Problem signal
Aircraft engines, life-limited parts (LLPs), rotables and structural components move between airlines, MROs, lessors, owners and buyers. Their operational status, installation/removal events, maintenance actions, modification status and release certificates must be reviewed and transferred. Inconsistent formats and paper/PDF archives can slow transitions and create record-review costs.

### Primary evidence and important correction
- The ATA e-Business Program's Aircraft Transfer Records Working Group maintains Spec 2500 for electronic aircraft/engine transfer records and is actively developing a Component Passport / back-to-birth component-history dataset. The proposed history includes manufacture/birth, operational data, installs, removals, maintenance, modifications and ownership changes.
- IATA's September 2026 LLP/TCC traceability guidance makes an important distinction: FAA/EASA rules require evidence of current status and relevant in-service history, but do not generally require a complete back-to-birth archive for determining current life status. IATA proposes the term **Current Status Confirmation (CSC)** to avoid unnecessary documentation demands and delays.
- EASA likewise says “back to birth” is not a regulatory term; LLP log cards and records needed to establish compliance/current status must be retained.
- Existing solutions include GE Aerospace Records Management System (digitisation, indexing, correction, search and M&E integration), FLYdocs (technical-records archive, audit and transitions), transition.aero (lease-transition records and outstanding-items workflows), TARA (records, digital signatures, ATA Spec 2000 exchange), and emerging AI audit products such as Botminds.

### Decision
**NO-GO for a generic aircraft component passport, immutable parts ledger, records archive, or AI records-audit platform.** ATA is already developing the relevant industry exchange standard; IATA is clarifying the minimum defensible LLP evidence; established vendors cover digitisation, indexing, transfer, audit and workflow. An onchain record cannot prove that an inspection or maintenance statement was true.

### Residual question (not a candidate)
Could independent LLP/rotable traders or lessors pay for a neutral, evidence-based CSC package that reduces unnecessary back-to-birth requests and accelerates a specific class of component transaction? This is not promoted to WATCH: no independent buyer interviews, transaction-level delay/cost figures, or willingness to pay were found. Reopen only with two independent lessors/traders/MROs providing redacted examples, measured delay/cost and a paid or time-boxed pilot path.

## 2. Battery second-life state-of-health and residual value

### Problem signal
Used EV and stationary batteries may be evaluated for residual value, reuse, repurposing, remanufacturing or recycling. A buyer or second-life operator needs credible state-of-health (SoH), lifetime, identity, operating history and provenance. BMS data is generated by different OEMs and may be commercially sensitive.

### Regulatory facts
- From 18 February 2027, the EU Battery Regulation requires a digital Battery Passport for each LMT battery, each industrial battery above 2 kWh and each EV battery placed on the market or put into service.
- Article 14 already requires up-to-date SoH / expected-lifetime parameters in the BMS for covered battery classes and read-only access for the legally entitled purchaser and relevant third parties for residual-value, further-use and repurposing purposes.
- The European Commission's FAQ says dynamic information must be updated when battery status changes or a change is being considered; frequent real-time updates are not universally required. Repurposed or remanufactured batteries receive a new passport linked to the previous passport.
- The Commission's registry became operational on 20 July 2026. Its FAQ says the passport itself is hosted by the responsible economic operator or an authorised provider; the registry primarily stores registration/metadata and facilitates authority access.
- The Commission has published a battery-passport provider landscape in which Circulor, Minespider, Circularise, Spherity, Siemens, osapiens, T-Systems, AVL and numerous lighter providers are active. Battery analytics / SoH providers form another adjacent competitive set.

### Decision
**NO-GO for generic Battery Passport, battery provenance ledger, SoH registry, or second-life marketplace.** The regulatory passport architecture, access framework and provider market are already taking shape. Article 14 provides a legal data-access basis, while OEM BMS integration and SoH analytics are specialist technical capabilities. No evidence establishes that a new shared chain is needed to make an SoH claim reliable.

### Residual question (not a candidate)
Could a specific second-life buyer, insurer or lender demonstrate that OEM BMS data access is legally available but operationally unusable across a defined set of battery models, creating measurable valuation haircuts or transaction failures? No such buyer-specific evidence was established. Do not promote to WATCH without actual transaction examples, independent battery-model coverage data, quantified haircut/inspection costs, and pilot willingness.

## 3. FuelEU Maritime pooling and settlement

### Problem signal
FuelEU Maritime creates positive and negative ship compliance balances. Pooling lets companies combine balances across ships and, where permitted, across companies. Parties negotiate commercial price, volume, payment terms, liability, surplus ownership and allocation. A supplier's failure to deliver agreed surplus can expose the buyer to a compliance deficit or penalty.

### Regulatory and market coverage
- The European Commission's FuelEU Q&A states that pooling is a private agreement between companies; each pool is overseen by one verifier, and participating companies register pool membership, allocation and verifier in the FuelEU database.
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/394 provides a FuelEU database pooling function for companies and verifiers to register and verify pool composition and balance allocation.
- OceanScore offers a pooling marketplace, surplus-price index, RFQs, counterparty discovery, commercial information and a separate compliance-management platform. Its platform materials also cover invoicing, charter-party cost allocation and off-hire handling.
- BetterSea markets a marketplace with more than 5,000 connected vessels and pooling, buy/sell surplus, commercial/legal transaction support. In September 2026 it announced integration with GTT Marine's Vesper Insights for trading, pooling, surplus index, simulation and post-trade / Thetis reporting.
- Zero44 markets FuelEU compliance management, tracking pooled/banked/borrowed balances, external pools and automated redistribution. Other providers include AHTI Climate, ACT Group, BSM, CarbonLeap, FincoEnergies, Gasum, Hecla, Prosmar and STX Group.

### Decision
**NO-GO for a generic FuelEU pool marketplace, balance registry, verifier workflow, or settlement ledger.** The EU database handles formal registration/verification, while OceanScore, BetterSea, Zero44 and other specialists already cover market discovery, pricing, pooling and compliance operations. There is no evidence that a new neutral ledger is required for commercial agreements or settlement.

### Residual question (not a candidate)
A narrow post-trade issue could remain around contractual failure, collateral/security, and allocation of penalties between shipowner, charterer, manager and surplus provider. But existing providers already market liability management and charter-party invoicing/off-hire handling. No independent examples quantify residual losses after those tools. Do not promote to WATCH without two operators providing failed-delivery or allocation-dispute cases, quantified exposure, and willingness to pilot.

## Arbitrum fit

None of the three generic propositions demonstrates a requirement for Arbitrum shared state:
- Aviation: ATA XML standards and signed records can exchange component status; the source evidence remains with approved operators and maintenance organizations.
- Batteries: the regulation mandates open, interoperable data exchange and restricted access, while the responsible economic operator hosts the passport. A public chain does not establish BMS truth or override confidentiality/access rules.
- FuelEU: the EU database and verifiers are authoritative for formal pool registration and allocation; commercial counterparties can contract and settle through existing channels.

A shared chain could become relevant only if a buyer demonstrates a multi-party dispute or transfer workflow where independent parties require a common, tamper-evident event record and existing standards, signed attestations, or central infrastructure cannot meet the need.
